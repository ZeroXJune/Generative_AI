LESSON: GENERAL CHEMISTRY - ATOMS, BONDING, AND REACTIONS

COURSE LEVEL: Undergraduate, first year
PREREQUISITES: Algebra, scientific notation

ATOMIC STRUCTURE
================
An atom consists of a nucleus containing protons and neutrons, surrounded by
electrons. The atomic number is the proton count and defines the element. The
mass number is protons plus neutrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element
with differing neutron counts.

Electrons occupy orbitals grouped into shells. The electron configuration
follows the Pauli exclusion principle (no two electrons share all four quantum
numbers), Hund's rule (orbitals of equal energy fill singly first), and the
Aufbau principle (lower energy orbitals fill first).

Valence electrons, those in the outermost shell, determine chemical behaviour.

THE PERIODIC TABLE
==================
Elements are arranged by increasing atomic number. Columns are groups, sharing
valence electron counts and therefore similar chemistry. Rows are periods.

Periodic trends moving left to right across a period:
- Atomic radius decreases, as increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons inward
- Ionisation energy increases
- Electronegativity increases

Moving down a group, atomic radius increases and ionisation energy decreases,
because valence electrons occupy shells further from the nucleus.

CHEMICAL BONDING
================
Ionic bonds form when electrons transfer between atoms of widely differing
electronegativity, typically a metal and a nonmetal, producing charged ions
held by electrostatic attraction.

Covalent bonds form when atoms share electron pairs. A nonpolar covalent bond
shares electrons roughly equally; a polar covalent bond shares them unevenly.

Metallic bonding involves a lattice of positive ions in a shared sea of
delocalised electrons, which explains electrical conductivity and malleability.

Intermolecular forces, weaker than bonds, include hydrogen bonding, dipole
interactions, and London dispersion forces. Hydrogen bonding explains water's
unusually high boiling point.

THE MOLE AND STOICHIOMETRY
==========================
The mole is the amount of substance containing approximately 6.022 times ten to
the twenty-third particles, known as Avogadro's number. Molar mass is the mass
in grams of one mole.

Stoichiometry uses balanced equations to relate quantities of reactants and
products. A balanced equation conserves both mass and charge.

ACIDS AND BASES
===============
An Arrhenius acid releases hydrogen ions in water; a base releases hydroxide
ions. The broader Bronsted-Lowry definition treats acids as proton donors and
bases as proton acceptors.

pH equals the negative base-ten logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration. A
pH of 7 is neutral at 25 degrees Celsius, below 7 acidic, above 7 basic. The pH
scale is logarithmic, so each unit represents a tenfold change in
concentration.

WHY IT MATTERS
==============
Chemistry explains materials, medicine, metabolism, and environmental
processes. Stoichiometry governs industrial yield; acid-base chemistry governs
biological buffering.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
=====================
- A balanced equation conserves atoms, not molecule counts
- Strong and concentrated are different: strength describes the degree of
  dissociation, concentration the amount per volume
- Bonds do not "store" energy that is released on breaking; breaking bonds
  requires energy, and forming them releases it
