LESSON: STATISTICS AND PROBABILITY - INFERENCE AND UNCERTAINTY

COURSE LEVEL: Undergraduate, first or second year
PREREQUISITES: Algebra II

DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS
======================
Measures of central tendency: the mean is the arithmetic average, the median
the middle value when ordered, the mode the most frequent value. The median
resists outliers; the mean does not.

Measures of spread: the range is maximum minus minimum. Variance is the average
squared deviation from the mean. Standard deviation is the square root of
variance and shares the units of the original data.

Distribution shape: a distribution is right-skewed when its tail extends
rightward, which pulls the mean above the median.

PROBABILITY FUNDAMENTALS
========================
Probability ranges from 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain).

- Addition rule for mutually exclusive events: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)
- General addition rule: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B)
- Multiplication rule for independent events: P(A and B) = P(A) times P(B)
- Conditional probability: P(A given B) = P(A and B) divided by P(B)

Bayes' theorem relates conditional probabilities in both directions:
P(A given B) equals P(B given A) times P(A), divided by P(B). It formalises how
evidence should update belief.

THE NORMAL DISTRIBUTION
=======================
The normal distribution is symmetric and bell-shaped, described entirely by its
mean and standard deviation. Under the empirical rule, approximately 68 percent
of observations lie within one standard deviation of the mean, 95 percent
within two, and 99.7 percent within three.

A z-score expresses how many standard deviations an observation lies from the
mean: the observation minus the mean, divided by the standard deviation.

THE CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM
=========================
The sampling distribution of the sample mean approaches a normal distribution
as sample size grows, regardless of the population's own shape, provided
observations are independent and the sample is sufficiently large. This is why
normal-based inference applies so widely.

The standard error of the mean is the population standard deviation divided by
the square root of the sample size. Note that quadrupling the sample size only
halves the standard error.

HYPOTHESIS TESTING
==================
The null hypothesis states there is no effect; the alternative states there is.
A test computes the probability of observing data at least as extreme as the
sample, assuming the null is true. That probability is the p-value.

A small p-value indicates the data would be unlikely under the null hypothesis.
A conventional threshold is 0.05, though the choice is a convention, not a law
of nature.

Errors: a Type I error rejects a true null (a false positive); a Type II error
fails to reject a false null (a false negative). Statistical power is the
probability of correctly detecting a real effect.

Confidence intervals give a range of plausible parameter values. A 95 percent
confidence interval means that across repeated sampling, 95 percent of such
intervals would contain the true parameter.

WHY IT MATTERS
==============
Statistics governs how evidence is evaluated in medicine, policy, and science.
Machine learning evaluation rests on it: accuracy, precision, recall, and
cross-validation are all statistical constructs.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
=====================
- A p-value is not the probability that the null hypothesis is true
- Statistical significance is not practical importance; a tiny effect can be
  significant in a large sample
- Correlation does not establish causation; confounding variables may explain
  an association
- Failing to reject the null does not prove the null is true
