LESSON: INTRODUCTORY PSYCHOLOGY - LEARNING, MEMORY, AND COGNITION

COURSE LEVEL: Undergraduate, first year
PREREQUISITES: None

WHAT PSYCHOLOGY IS
==================
Psychology is the scientific study of behaviour and mental processes. It relies
on empirical methods: experiments, correlational studies, case studies, and
observation.

Major perspectives include the biological (brain and genetics), behavioural
(observable responses), cognitive (mental processing), humanistic (growth and
self-actualisation), and sociocultural.

RESEARCH METHODS
================
An experiment manipulates an independent variable and measures a dependent
variable, with random assignment to conditions. Random assignment is what
permits causal conclusions.

Correlational studies measure association without manipulation and therefore
cannot establish causation. A correlation coefficient ranges from negative one
to positive one; zero indicates no linear relationship.

Control measures include placebo conditions, blinding, and representative
sampling. The placebo effect demonstrates that expectation alone can change
outcomes.

CLASSICAL CONDITIONING
======================
Discovered by Ivan Pavlov, classical conditioning pairs a neutral stimulus with
one that already produces a response.

In Pavlov's work, food was the unconditioned stimulus and salivation the
unconditioned response. After repeated pairing with a bell, the bell became a
conditioned stimulus producing a conditioned response.

Related processes: extinction (the response fades when pairing stops),
spontaneous recovery, generalisation to similar stimuli, and discrimination
between them.

OPERANT CONDITIONING
====================
Developed by B. F. Skinner, operant conditioning shapes behaviour through
consequences.

- Positive reinforcement adds a desirable consequence
- Negative reinforcement removes an unpleasant one
- Positive punishment adds an unpleasant consequence
- Negative punishment removes a desirable one

Note that "negative" means removal, not undesirable. Both forms of
reinforcement increase behaviour.

Reinforcement schedules matter: variable ratio schedules, which reward after an
unpredictable number of responses, produce the most persistent behaviour. This
is the mechanism behind gambling.

MEMORY
======
The three-stage model describes sensory memory (brief, large capacity),
short-term or working memory (roughly seven items, seconds to a minute without
rehearsal), and long-term memory (durable, effectively unlimited).

Encoding moves information into storage; retrieval brings it back. Elaborative
rehearsal, which connects new material to existing knowledge, outperforms rote
repetition.

Forgetting arises from decay, interference (where other memories compete), and
retrieval failure. The spacing effect shows that distributed practice produces
better retention than massed practice, which is why cramming performs poorly on
delayed tests.

COGNITIVE BIASES
================
- Confirmation bias: favouring evidence that supports existing beliefs
- Availability heuristic: judging frequency by how easily examples come to mind
- Anchoring: over-relying on the first piece of information encountered
- Hindsight bias: believing an outcome was predictable after the fact

WHY IT MATTERS
==============
Psychology informs education, clinical treatment, workplace design, and public
health messaging. Its findings on spacing and retrieval practice have direct
implications for how to study.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
=====================
- Negative reinforcement is not punishment; it increases behaviour
- Memory does not record events like video; it reconstructs them, and
  reconstruction introduces error
- Most psychological traits reflect both genetics and environment rather than
  one alone
