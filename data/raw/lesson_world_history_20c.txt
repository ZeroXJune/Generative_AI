LESSON: WORLD HISTORY - THE TWENTIETH CENTURY

COURSE LEVEL: Undergraduate, first year
PREREQUISITES: None

THE FIRST WORLD WAR, 1914-1918
==============================
Long-term causes are conventionally grouped as militarism, alliance systems,
imperial competition, and nationalism. The immediate trigger was the
assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria-Hungary in Sarajevo in
June 1914.

The alliance system turned a regional crisis into a continental war. The
Central Powers comprised Germany, Austria-Hungary, and the Ottoman Empire; the
Allied Powers included France, Britain, Russia, and from 1917 the United
States.

Trench warfare on the Western Front produced static lines and enormous
casualties. Machine guns, artillery, and poison gas gave defenders decisive
advantage over attacking infantry.

The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 1919, imposed territorial losses,
reparations, and military restrictions on Germany, and assigned war guilt.
Historians widely regard its terms as a contributing factor to later German
grievance.

THE RUSSIAN REVOLUTION, 1917
============================
Two revolutions occurred in 1917. The February Revolution ended Tsarist rule
and produced a provisional government. The October Revolution brought the
Bolsheviks, led by Vladimir Lenin, to power. Civil war followed, and the Soviet
Union was formally established in 1922.

THE INTERWAR PERIOD
===================
The Great Depression began with the United States stock market crash of October
1929 and spread internationally through trade and banking linkages.
Unemployment reached roughly a quarter of the workforce in the United States.

Economic collapse and political instability aided the rise of authoritarian
movements: Fascism in Italy under Mussolini from 1922, and National Socialism
in Germany, with Hitler appointed Chancellor in January 1933.

THE SECOND WORLD WAR, 1939-1945
===============================
The war in Europe began with the German invasion of Poland in September 1939.
The Pacific war expanded following the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor in
December 1941, which brought the United States into the conflict.

The Holocaust was the systematic, state-organised genocide of approximately six
million European Jews, alongside the mass murder of Roma, disabled people,
political prisoners, and others.

The war in Europe ended in May 1945 with German surrender. The war in the
Pacific ended in August 1945 following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and
Nagasaki and the Soviet entry into the Pacific war.

Total deaths are estimated between seventy and eighty-five million, the
majority civilian.

THE COLD WAR, 1947-1991
=======================
A prolonged geopolitical rivalry between the United States and the Soviet Union,
conducted through proxy conflicts, arms competition, and ideological contest
rather than direct war between the principals.

Key episodes include the Berlin Blockade, the Korean War, the Cuban Missile
Crisis of 1962, and the Vietnam War. The period ended with the fall of the
Berlin Wall in 1989 and the dissolution of the Soviet Union in December 1991.

DECOLONISATION
==============
Following 1945, European empires dissolved across Asia and Africa. India and
Pakistan gained independence in 1947. Most African states became independent
during the 1950s and 1960s. Borders drawn by colonial administrations, often
disregarding existing ethnic and linguistic boundaries, contributed to later
conflicts.

WHY IT MATTERS
==============
Contemporary international institutions, borders, and alliances were largely
shaped in this century. The United Nations, the European Union, and NATO are
all direct products of its conflicts.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
=====================
- The Versailles Treaty did not single-handedly cause the Second World War;
  economic collapse and political choices were also decisive
- The United States entered both world wars years after they began
- The Cold War involved extensive direct fighting, but in proxy theatres rather
  than between the superpowers themselves
