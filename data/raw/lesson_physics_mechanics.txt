LESSON: CLASSICAL MECHANICS - MOTION, FORCES, AND ENERGY

COURSE LEVEL: Undergraduate, first year
PREREQUISITES: Algebra, basic trigonometry, introductory calculus helpful

KINEMATICS
==========
Kinematics describes motion without reference to its causes.

Displacement is the change in position, a vector quantity. Velocity is the rate
of change of displacement; speed is its magnitude. Acceleration is the rate of
change of velocity.

For constant acceleration in one dimension:
- v = v0 + a*t
- x = x0 + v0*t + (1/2)*a*t^2
- v^2 = v0^2 + 2*a*(x - x0)

Near Earth's surface, gravitational acceleration is approximately 9.81 metres
per second squared, directed downward.

Projectile motion decomposes into independent horizontal and vertical
components. Horizontal velocity stays constant when air resistance is
neglected; vertical motion undergoes constant downward acceleration.

NEWTON'S LAWS OF MOTION
=======================
First law (inertia): an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion
continues at constant velocity, unless acted on by a net external force.

Second law: the net force on an object equals its mass times its acceleration,
F = m*a. Force is measured in newtons, where one newton is one kilogram metre
per second squared.

Third law: for every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. Forces
occur in pairs acting on different objects.

Common forces: weight (mass times gravitational acceleration), normal force
perpendicular to a surface, friction opposing relative motion, and tension
along a rope or cable.

WORK, ENERGY, AND POWER
=======================
Work is force times displacement in the direction of the force, measured in
joules. Kinetic energy is one half times mass times velocity squared.
Gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface is mass times
gravitational acceleration times height.

The work-energy theorem states that the net work done on an object equals its
change in kinetic energy.

Conservation of energy: in a closed system with only conservative forces, the
total mechanical energy remains constant. Friction converts mechanical energy
into thermal energy, which is why it is called non-conservative.

Power is the rate of energy transfer, measured in watts, where one watt is one
joule per second.

MOMENTUM
========
Linear momentum is mass times velocity. Impulse is force times the time over
which it acts, and equals the change in momentum.

Conservation of momentum: in the absence of external forces, the total momentum
of a system is constant. This holds in all collisions.

Elastic collisions conserve both momentum and kinetic energy. Inelastic
collisions conserve momentum but not kinetic energy; perfectly inelastic
collisions leave the objects moving together.

WHY IT MATTERS
==============
Mechanics is the foundation of engineering, from structural analysis to
spacecraft trajectories. Its conservation laws generalise into every later
branch of physics.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
=====================
- Motion does not require a continuing force; constant velocity requires zero
  net force
- Mass and weight differ: mass is an intrinsic quantity in kilograms, weight is
  a force in newtons that depends on local gravity
- Action-reaction pairs act on different objects and therefore never cancel
