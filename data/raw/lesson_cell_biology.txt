LESSON: CELL BIOLOGY - STRUCTURE, DIVISION, AND GENE EXPRESSION

COURSE LEVEL: Undergraduate, first year
PREREQUISITES: High school biology, basic chemistry

CELL THEORY
===========
Three principles form the foundation of cell biology: all living organisms are
composed of one or more cells; the cell is the basic unit of structure and
function; and all cells arise from pre-existing cells.

PROKARYOTES AND EUKARYOTES
==========================
Prokaryotic cells, comprising bacteria and archaea, lack a membrane-bound
nucleus. Their DNA sits in the cytoplasm in a region called the nucleoid. They
are typically one to five micrometres across.

Eukaryotic cells, found in animals, plants, fungi, and protists, enclose their
DNA within a nucleus and contain membrane-bound organelles. They are typically
ten to one hundred micrometres across.

MAJOR ORGANELLES
================
- Nucleus: houses chromosomes; site of DNA replication and transcription
- Mitochondria: generate ATP through cellular respiration; contain their own
  circular DNA, evidence for the endosymbiotic theory
- Ribosomes: synthesise proteins by translating messenger RNA
- Endoplasmic reticulum: the rough ER studded with ribosomes processes
  proteins; the smooth ER synthesises lipids
- Golgi apparatus: modifies, sorts, and packages proteins for transport
- Lysosomes: contain hydrolytic enzymes that degrade waste and worn organelles
- Chloroplasts: in plants and algae, conduct photosynthesis

The plasma membrane is a phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins, described
by the fluid mosaic model. It is selectively permeable.

TRANSPORT ACROSS MEMBRANES
==========================
Passive transport requires no energy and moves substances down a concentration
gradient. Simple diffusion moves small nonpolar molecules directly; facilitated
diffusion uses channel or carrier proteins. Osmosis is the diffusion of water
across a semipermeable membrane.

Active transport moves substances against a gradient and consumes ATP. The
sodium-potassium pump exports three sodium ions for every two potassium ions
imported, maintaining the membrane potential.

CELL DIVISION
=============
Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells. Its stages
are prophase (chromosomes condense), metaphase (chromosomes align at the
equator), anaphase (sister chromatids separate), and telophase (nuclei
re-form), followed by cytokinesis.

Meiosis produces four genetically distinct haploid gametes through two
successive divisions. Crossing over during prophase I and independent
assortment during metaphase I generate genetic diversity.

THE CENTRAL DOGMA
=================
Genetic information flows from DNA to RNA to protein.

Transcription, occurring in the nucleus, copies a DNA template into messenger
RNA via RNA polymerase. Translation, occurring at the ribosome, reads mRNA in
three-base codons and assembles the corresponding amino acid chain.

The genetic code uses 64 codons to specify 20 amino acids plus stop signals, so
the code is redundant but not ambiguous: several codons may specify one amino
acid, but no codon specifies two.

WHY IT MATTERS
==============
Cell biology underlies medicine, genetics, and biotechnology. Cancer is
fundamentally a disorder of cell cycle regulation; antibiotics exploit
differences between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells.

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
=====================
- Mitosis is one phase of the cell cycle, not the whole of it; interphase
  occupies most of a cell's life
- Osmosis concerns water movement, not solute movement
- DNA is not translated directly into protein; RNA is the required intermediate
